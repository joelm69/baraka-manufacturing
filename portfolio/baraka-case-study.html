Sales growth sped up from 5.8% in 2024 to 7.7% in 2025, but profit grew only 1.4% and then 4.3%. Margin slipped from 26.9% in 2023 to 25.8% and then 25.0%. The rest of this page explains why, by answering the eight questions in the brief.

The brief

Baraka Manufacturing sells six products (cooking oil, maize flour, bar soap, detergent, mango juice and tissue) to five customer segments across seven markets in Kenya, Uganda, Rwanda and Tanzania. I received 6,264 monthly sales records and 180 monthly targets for 2023–2025, and was asked to build a dashboard and a one-page note for the CFO.

Question 8 of 8

What would you change in the discount policy?

  1. Cap the High band, starting with Distributors. Allow High discounts only for clear conditions such as volume tiers or early payment. Aim to bring Distributors' High-band share down from 41% toward 10–15%.
  2. Set a margin floor. Any deal below about 15% margin needs finance sign-off. The High band currently averages 12.4% and has fallen every year.
  3. Review discounting monthly. Track discount % and High-band share by segment on the dashboard, so a shift like June–July 2025 is caught within the month instead of at year end.
Expected impact

Moving half of Distributors' High-band sales to the Medium band would add roughly KES 0.4M of profit a year, assuming volume holds. Some Distributors may buy less, but at a 10% margin that trade-off is worth testing.

How I built it

Data model

Sales and targets are recorded at different levels of detail. Each sale is a month, segment, market, product and discount band (6,264 rows). Each target is just a month and segment (180 rows). My first attempt joined them on segment, which copied every sale once for each month's target and produced 225,504 rows (6,264 × 36), inflating every total.

I fixed it by unioning the two tables into one (6,444 rows), so each value exists once, then relating that table to shared Date, Segment, Market and Product tables. One filter now controls both sales and targets. Target rows have no market or product, so selecting a market hides the plan charts; the dashboard says so in a note.

Measures and time intelligence

  • A measure library of named calculations (Total Sales, Gross Sales, Total Discounts, Discount %, COGS, Total Profit, Profit Margin, Units Sold, Target Sales, Variance to Target, Variance %), so every chart uses one definition.
  • Margins are calculated as a ratio of totals (SUM(profit) / SUM(sales)), never as an average of row-level margins.
  • A Selected Year parameter drives current-year and last-year measures such as SUM(IF YEAR([month]) = [Selected Year] - 1 THEN [Sales] END). A normal year filter would have removed last year's rows and broken every comparison.
  • The fact table's raw month, segment, market and product columns are hidden. They duplicate the shared dimension tables, and using them would stop filters from reaching the targets.

Design choices

  • Colour carries meaning only: green for good, orange for bad, charcoal and grey for everything else. Colour is tied to stable fields so it stays correct when the year changes.
  • The variance heatmap's diverging palette is centred at zero, so grey always means "on plan".
  • Click a segment to filter the Margin Drivers page; click any mark to highlight it across all charts.

Limitations

  • Profit here is gross profit: sales minus discounts minus cost of goods. Overheads and tax are not in the data.
  • Targets exist only by segment and month. I recommend setting market-level targets from 2026.
  • 2023 is the first year of data, so growth comparisons start in 2024.
  • The discount-policy impact assumes customers keep buying the same volume.
